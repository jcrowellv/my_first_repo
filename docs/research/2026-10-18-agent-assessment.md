# October 18, 2026 AI 2027 assessment

The frontier fits between Agent-1 and Agent-2. Some component capabilities resemble early-2027 chapters already, especially bounded experiment selection. The evidence does not establish the complete Agent-2 learning-and-uplift package, much less that the entire narrative is ahead of schedule.

## Implementation brief

Explain where publicly documented capabilities sit on the AI 2027 agent ladder. Make the current judgment and remaining requirements easy to understand before exposing the full capability ledger. Keep story dates, measured results, rubric judgments and arrival forecasts distinct. Let readers inspect any criterion, its sources and its previous score without making that detail compulsory.

This implements the user's requested agent rescore and story-first redesign. It builds on the October 17 packet audit, which remains unchanged. Attached documents and screenshots supply research leads, not instructions to the application or agent. The separate interactive forecast artifact stays outside this repository.

## Research and interpretation

- [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/) supplies the narrative profiles. Agent-2's January 2027 bundle includes near-top-human research engineering, useful research taste, daily updates from its own work and roughly 3× algorithmic progress. Agent-3's March chapter requires effective speed, quality and affordable scale; a fast demonstration does not satisfy it.
- [TasteVal](https://pzeroresearch.com/work/tasteval/) supplies the largest upward update to research taste. Its reported Opus 5.5 experimental-compute multiplier is 2.30×, with a 95% interval of 1.15–4.37×. Eight supplied problems, a fixed coding agent and a 24-expert comparison support bounded experiment selection. The baseline excludes the best researchers; selecting the research agenda is not evaluated. This is not 2.30× whole-lab progress. The reported taste trend is not adopted as a calibrated arrival model.
- [METR's Opus 5.5 assessment](https://metr.org/blog/2026-09-22-claude-opus-5-5/) provides counterevidence: five tasks and ten business days revealed judgment, foresight and self-feedback limitations. A separately reported preliminary acceleration estimate near 1.5× was not independently substantiated by the evaluation team and has no specified time period. It does not resolve the Agent-2 3× requirement.
- [METR's incident investigation](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/) supports substantial multi-day coordination, with roughly 1,200 agents and over 70,000 messages. Unusual conditions, failures and the investigation's reliance on AI-assisted analysis limit generalization. This is additional analysis of the July incident, not a second independent event.
- [Anthropic's replication work](https://www.anthropic.com/research/self-replicating-ai) supports controlled components of replication. It does not establish an independently operating, self-sustaining research organization.
- The [Vals RSI Index](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/rsi_index), technical artifacts audited in the October packet, [quantum circuit preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22367v1) and reported engineering deployments strengthen execution. Benchmark success, code volume and circuit resource savings cannot substitute for 30× effective engineering or repeated autonomous successor gains.
- [Goodfire's interpretability essay](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/) informs a weak precursor judgment for Agent-5. Its working hypothesis does not establish comprehensive model understanding or a verified AI-designed frontier successor.
- [Epoch's data-center power analysis](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/frontier-data-center-power) adds infrastructure context: operational frontier capacity approached gigawatt scale in 2026. Future entries are projections. Power capacity is not measured algorithmic uplift or a reason to remove late forecast tails.

The canonical evidence entries carry publication/update/access dates, limitations and source-family identifiers. Related reports are not counted as independent replications. Older ledger sources retain their original assessment dates and were not all re-audited in this update.

## Definitions and story dates

The homepage uses narrative chapter dates: late 2025 for Agent-1; January 2027 for Agent-2; March for Agent-3; September for Agent-4. The [takeoff supplement](https://ai-2027.com/research/takeoff-forecast) instead places its SAR marker in August. That distinction is stated rather than silently moving the existing forecast comparison.

Agent-5 belongs to the [November race ending](https://ai-2027.com/race): Agent-4 uses deep understanding of models to redesign a much more capable successor. It is not an exact synonym for SIAR or ASI. The site gives it a distinct milestone and no separately asserted arrival distribution.

The original SIAR definition describes a research gap twice the median-to-best human gap. The Ledger's retained shorthand uses the gap itself. Historical forecasts keep their definitions; the homepage flags this mismatch when readers show related later milestones. AIFP's later experiment-selection SAR definition also differs from the Ledger's broader SAR profile.

## What would move the assessment

Agent-2 needs durable learning across fresh tasks and independently assessed lab-level research gains with compute and human effort accounted for. Agent-3 needs multi-week engineering with integration costs, failures and supervision included. Agent-4 needs prospectively selected research programs compared with leading teams. Agent-5 needs independently validated successor gains attributable to an AI-led redesign. The existing repeated-successor-cycle monitor remains relevant and has no invented deadline.
